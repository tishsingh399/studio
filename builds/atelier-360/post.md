## Lettering + lino account

Different medium this month. I built a room.

It's the studio I'd want: chair sketches pinned to the wall, thumbnails on paper, a pencil left next to the ruler. You stand at the desk and drag to look around.

The fiddly part was the one spot nobody is meant to notice. A 360° picture is a flat strip bent into a circle, and where the two ends meet you get a hard line. It felt a lot like registering a second colour on a print. If the edges don't sit on top of each other, that's all anyone sees.

I blended the last 40 pixels on each side into each other. It's still not perfect if you go hunting for it.

## UX with intent

I put my portfolio in a room. You land at a desk, turn around, and the things in the room open each section: design systems, case studies, AI work, about.

The one thing I learned: a 360° panorama is a 2:1 image wrapped around you, so the left and right edges have to match or you see a seam every time you spin past the back wall. I wrote a tiny script that blends the outer band of each edge toward the other side. It's in my studio repo if you want it.

The part I care about more: the room is optional. Without JavaScript or WebGL it's a plain list of the same links, the keyboard works, and reduced motion turns off the easing.

I'm honestly not sure a room is a better way to read a portfolio than a page. It was more fun to make, though.
